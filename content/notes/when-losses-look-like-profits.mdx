I have been evaluating a tool that scrapes quarterly financial reports from the Indonesia Stock Exchange and turns them into clean CSVs for stock screening. The idea is solid: official XBRL filings instead of reading PDFs one by one.

While testing the parser, I found four bugs. The scariest one was in how XBRL represents negative numbers: the sign lives in an attribute on the tag, while the text itself holds the absolute value. The parser ignored the attribute. Every loss in every filing was read as a profit.

Imagine screening for profitable companies with that bug in place. The worst performers would top your list.

The other three were less dramatic but the same flavor: annual filings that were downloaded but never parsed, a whole taxonomy mapping that could never trigger, and share counts silently overwritten with zero. Each fix was small. Together they changed every number downstream.

Two more issues surfaced during review and are still open: thin coverage for insurance companies, and numbers with thousand separators failing silently. Data pipelines fail quietly more often than loudly, and that is what makes them dangerous.

The tool is worth using now. But I will never quite trust a financial CSV I have not spot-checked again.
