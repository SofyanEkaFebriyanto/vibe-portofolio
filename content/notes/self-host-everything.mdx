Everything I build runs on a small ARM set-top box sitting in my room. It hosts my AI gateway, my voice assistant, this portfolio, and a tunnel that exposes it all.

## Why not just use the cloud?

Cost is the obvious answer, but it is not the real one. The real answer is ownership. When something breaks at night, I can log in and fix it. No dashboards, no support tickets, no surprise bills. I know every process running on that box.

## Constraints are a feature

Limited RAM forces discipline. My Go binaries are tiny. My portfolio is static files. There is no room for bloat, so bloat never arrives. Every service earns its place.

Small disk taught me what actually matters: code and data. Caches are regenerable. Logs rotate. If it cannot be rebuilt, it gets backed up.

## What it taught me

Self-hosting turned deployment from a scary word into a habit. Pull, build, restart. Done in a minute. That loop is the reason I ship as often as I do.

You do not need expensive infrastructure to build real things. You need a box you understand.
